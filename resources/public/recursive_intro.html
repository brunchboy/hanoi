The Recursive Algorithm

First, let’s demystify these words…

Algorithm
In mathematics and computer science, any well-defined set of instructions that, when followed, terminates after a finite number of steps, that comprise a solution to a given problem.

The word comes from the name of the Persian scientist and polymath Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (محمد بن موسى الخوارزميّ) who wrote, around 825 CE, a “Book of Indian computation” and “Addition and subtraction in Indian arithmetic” among others, which were widely translated.

The latinization of al-Khwārizmī’s name was Algoritmi, making him the origin of this important word.
Recursion
In mathematics and computer science, a class of methods defined by two properties:
  • A simple base case (or cases)—a scenario that does not use recursion to produce an answer.
  • A recurive step—a set of rules that reduces towards the base case.
    • As an example, a recursive definition of ancestor is: either your parent (base case), or your parent’s ancestor (recursive step).
